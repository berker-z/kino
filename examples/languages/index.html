Three languages

Three proofs of concept, each a new operation rather than a new palette: a double exposure with a film treatment, browser-set type revealed as geometry, and a frame built from strips of different moments. Each has its own composition, a cross-material A/B, browser tests, and a critique. Rendered at 2560×1440.

Videos are local files next to this page (gitignored). Started from commit 5a48ebd.

The Silent City

A marble head comes up out of black. A refinery at night rises inside her silhouette, plumes drifting up through the head, then dissolves into a harbour's lights in fog. Two lines of type rise into the black at the right. One shot, 12 s, 24 fps.

compositions/xp-darkroomrender 55 s92 MB (grain)

Cross-material: raw · ditherPass · screenPass (cyanotype)

Reusable

NEWdoubleExposure (arrange): foreground, interior and mask as three painters, alpha or luma coverage, screen or replace. Output is opaque RGB, so any pass takes it. The A/B is the proof: the same frames raw, Bayer-dithered and on the Trinitron with no change to the scene.

NEWfilmPass: halation thresholded before it's blurred, toe and S-curve, midtone-weighted grain re-seeded per frame, weave, split tone. strength: 0 is an exact passthrough.

USEDhtmlPlate + lineRise for the title, the typesetting primitives recombined.

Composition-only

The plate prep, framing, the screen-weighted optical dissolve, the 0.3-stop exposure lift and all timings. The mask was cut offline by make-mask.py (warmth + brightness against the grey sweep); it's a fixture, not segmentation.

Plates: a Getty bust of Livia (CC0), a refinery at night (CC BY-SA 3.0, Uxud), Preemraff Lysekil in fog (CC0). Credits in assets/CREDITS.txt.

Critique

Works: you read one image living in another's shape at once, and the 14% of marble left over the refinery keeps it from looking cut out. The halation on the harbour lights does the "exposed, not overlaid" job better than any of the compositing. It holds a lot of black, which the brief asked for.

Weaker: silhouette-with-a-landscape-inside is the True Detective title, and it's become a trope; this is a well-made version of a known thing. In the harbour section the upper head goes flat grey (the fog sky is empty), and the bust's sawn-off back makes a straight vertical edge. The type is very polite. Grain at 1440p makes the encode huge.

Don't promote: the dissolve and the exposure lift. A darkroom look waits for your verdict on the treatment.

The Name of the Thing

An almost empty page: a marker, a hairline rule drawing across, then construction lines (cap height, baseline, a hairline through every stem). The word is drafted in construction order: every stem drops in left to right, then the bars, then the curves and diagonals. The guides fade, two columns rise line by line, and the page holds while the word's plane drifts 1.2% toward you. 12 s, 30 fps, no material.

Take two. Take one assembled the word from shuffled rectangular slabs; you found it too random.

compositions/xp-typesettingrender 18 s

Cross-material: risoPass, word and rule in one drum, copy in the other

Reusable

NEWhtmlPlate data-lines: one box per line where the browser broke it.

NEWlineRise: lines entering from below behind their own boxes. Already used by all three pieces.

NEWletterStrokes / drawStroke + htmlPlate data-chars: each letter's ink sorted into stems (near-full-height columns), bars (long horizontal runs through a stem) and curves (the rest), read from the browser's raster. All pieces together are the intact word, pixel for pixel.

KEPTfragmentGrid and friends, from take one; unused now.

Composition-only

The page (CSS grid, Inter Tight 500 at 380 px tracked 0.3em, IBM Plex Mono captions), the schedule, and drawing the word into its own layer so the push moves one intact raster. No letter spacing or line breaking is done in canvas anywhere.

Critique

Works: paused at any frame it still looks typeset, because it is; motion only chooses what's visible. The assembly has the patience the brief wanted, and the line rise is calm and exact. The riso A/B needed no change to the type source.

Weaker: wide-tracked Inter at monumental size is a default "premium" look. O and S have no stems or bars, so they arrive whole at the end. Where a curve meets a stem a small nub sometimes travels with the stem (the K's junction). The final push is so slow it's mostly there to keep the frame alive.

An Object Outside Time

A small world that's a pure function of its own time: a colonnade sliding past, a mast with a long bar turning on it, a train crossing. Vertical strips open to 1.1 s of time difference and the bar bends into a curve; the scan hands over to horizontal bands through a nested scan, and the train leans back through time while the bar becomes an S. 9 s, 30 fps, ice on navy.

compositions/xp-temporal-scanrender 9 s (256 strips)

Cross-material: ditherPass, Bayer 8, ice in three tones

Reusable

NEWtemporalScan (arrange) and scanStrips: strip i samples t − offset − span·u. Stateless, no frame cache. With span 0 it's the source. The sampler knows nothing about the material; the dither A/B just wraps it.

Composition-only

The world, the schedule, and nesting one scan inside another for the axis handover (a pattern, not an option).

Performance

The whole 9 s rendered in 9 s of wall time at 2560×1440: 256 strips for the x scan, 180 bands for y, 48 × 96 nested during the handover. No buffers are allocated beyond the output canvas. That's for a cheap procedural source; a photo or footage source would need a bounded cache of sampled frames (14 MB each at 1440p), which isn't built.

Critique

Works: the bending bar is unmistakably several moments at once, and the colonnade re-spacing makes the direction legible. Nesting means the axis change crosses rather than cuts.

Weaker: it's a diagram, not a piece yet. The train with windows and wheels is clip-art (it replaced a stick figure that was worse). At the bar's fastest moment small steps between strips still show. Photographic material through it is the obvious next test.

Verdict

Darkroom is the most promising piece. The temporal scan is the most interesting operation. Darkroom already looks like a title sequence; the scan is a genuinely new capability whose imagery is the weakest of the three. Typesetting is the most immediately useful: every future piece has type in it.

The primitive each one actually found

  • Darkroom: doubleExposure. The film pass is good, but the reusable idea is the explicit mask contract (alpha vs luma, opaque out) that lets a silhouette composite go into any material.
  • Typesetting: lineRise plus data-lines. The slab fragments are a good grammar but narrower.
  • Temporal scan: temporalScan, stateless and cheap enough for 256 strips at 1440p.

Public now vs later

Public now (exported in window.Kino): doubleExposure, filmPass, temporalScan, lineRise, htmlPlate lines. Each is the operation its brief asked for or is already used by more than one piece.

Exported but provisional until a second piece uses them: fragmentGrid, inkedFragments, drawFragments.

Not promoted: the optical dissolve, scan nesting, mask cutting. No new MotionAesthetic; a darkroom look waits for your verdict.

The brief's questions

The (ctx, t) idiom held for all three; the scan's source only gained a third argument (its strip) for culling. Double exposure is three painters in, one painter out, with scratch canvases hidden inside. Alpha lives in masks and stops at the composite, which is opaque, so no pass changed. Typesetting needed htmlPlate plus one measurement and two small functions. The scan needed nothing from any renderer. Recombined with existing pieces: dither, cyanotype screen and riso all took the new output unchanged.
