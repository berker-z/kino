The Doré recipe,
taken apart

One ImageMagick command, one fallen angel. What the command actually does, how it holds up on things that aren't engravings, what else can stand in for it, and how each method behaves once the picture moves.

magick in.jpg -colorspace gray -resize 50% -sigmoidal-contrast 4x50% \
  -dither FloydSteinberg -monochrome +level-colors '#16120e','#5c4c39' \
  -filter point -resize 200% out.png

Sources: eight Doré engravings (public domain), our Commons plates, and ten random photos (CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA). Credits in media/src/CREDITS.txt and media/src/misc/CREDITS.txt. Everything here is regenerated by exp.py. Click any image for full size.

01The recipe is a threshold

First, a sanity check: the reference image is Doré's Satan falling, Paradise Lost plate 12. Fitting the scan to 1920 wide and cropping 1080 tall from y = 330 matches the reference (correlation 0.93), and the recipe on that crop reproduces it almost pixel for pixel.

Then the surprise. Run the same command on a smooth gradient and there is no dithering at all: a hard cut at 50%. In ImageMagick 7, -monochrome ignores -dither. Swap it for -remap or -colors 2 and you get real Floyd-Steinberg.

So the texture in the reference isn't dither. It's Doré's own engraved lines, cut at a threshold at half resolution. The horizontal streaks are his burin, a little aliased by the half-size step. That's why it looks so good on an engraving, and why it can't work the same way on a photo.

02The recipe on everything

Engravings first, then photos. On photos there are no lines to cut, so you get flat posterized blobs: strong silhouettes, no texture. Some of them are good as graphics (the night diner, the statue, the spacewalk); faces and soft subjects fall apart.

03Helping photos

Local contrast (CLAHE) before the threshold brings out more edges, so photos read better, but it's still a cut-out, not an engraving.

04Real dithering

The same photos through actual error diffusion and its relatives. All of them keep the tones and the face. With two dark colours the result is murky at this size; zoom in and it's a fine grain. Floyd-Steinberg and Stucki are smooth, Atkinson keeps more contrast (it throws away a quarter of the error), Bayer is the regular grid of old Macs and games, blue noise is the grainiest. ImageMagick's Riemersma hits the same -monochrome threshold as the recipe.

05Fake engraving: the one that works on photos

If the look comes from engraved lines, give photos lines. Each pixel is compared against a field of parallel lines; dark areas swallow more of each line, so line width follows tone, the way a burin cuts deeper in shadow. Bent pushes the line phase with a blurred copy of the picture, so the lines ride over forms instead of running straight through them: banknote engraving, in about ten lines of numpy.

This is the one that makes random photos look like they belong next to the Doré. It works on faces, landscapes and objects alike. The bent version is the closest to a hand engraving; the straight one is cleaner and more graphic. Both are one threshold per pixel, so they're cheap enough for a per-frame pass.

06Pixel size

The recipe's 50% then 200% makes every dither pixel 2×2 screen pixels. 1× is finer than the reference and loses the chunkiness; 3× and 4× start to read as pixel art. 2× is right at 1080p; at 1440p, 2× or 3×.

07Palettes

Two colours carry the whole mood. The sepia pair is low contrast on purpose: it reads as old paper in a dark room. Lighter grounds (bone, paper and ink) turn it into print.

08In motion

The real question for video. Each clip is the same slow push-in, rendered per frame by each method. The number is how many pixels flip between consecutive frames: some flipping is the picture actually moving, the rest is boil.

portrait · Bayer 8×81.1%the picture slides under a fixed grid
Error diffusion boils. Every tiny change upstream moves every pixel after it, so a quarter of the frame changes every frame. That's the "random static" we've ruled out. Use Floyd-Steinberg for stills and holds only. Anything that moves wants a threshold against something stable: the engraving's own lines, a line screen, or a Bayer matrix.

09What I'd build

  • engravePass: the fake engraving as a kino pass. Any grayscale source in, two colours out, with the lines either fixed to the screen (calm, steady) or carried by the picture (filmic). Line period, angle and bend as settings; tone stretch built in. This is the new thing.
  • A dore look: the sepia pair, 2× pixels, a threshold mode for real engravings (the recipe, done right), and the engrave pass for everything else.
  • Error diffusion in ditherCanvas as a still-only mode, for title cards and freeze frames, with a note that it boils in motion.
  • Then a short piece: Doré plates and random photos mixed in one engraving language, camera moving slowly across them. That proves whether a photo and a real engraving can share a frame.
