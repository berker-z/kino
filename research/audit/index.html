A look today = material (carried in the look object) + motion grammar (in prose and in which arrangements a composition picks) + typography and palette. A composition chooses all three, and none of them is locked to the others.

03The experiments

Each one takes ingredients from different looks and counts the glue. No library code changed for any of them.

A2: blueprint's drawings, cyanotype's material, cyanotype's motion the full test

Source from blueprint (the self-drawing pen kit). Material from cyanotype (the screen). Motion grammar from cyanotype too: a contact strip sliding at constant speed, a torn-print wipe, a slow push. Nothing from blueprint's sheet camera.

Glue: about 12 lines. printStrip expects pre-rendered prints but reads them at paint time, so redrawing each card every frame keeps the drawings drafting while they move. The pen kit draws with whatever stroke style the caller sets, so that's set once per card.

A: material swap only half the test

Pen kit under cyanotype, but still with blueprint's sheet camera. It works, and it showed one real thing: at wide zoom, the cyanotype blur and grille thin the pen lines to threads.

B: arrangement swap works

Four teletext pages laid out as one wall, with blueprint's sheet camera gliding between them instead of teletext's page search. Glue: one translate per page.

Your test: if it works with a small amount of glue, the modularity is real. It does: about a dozen lines, nothing copied out of a composition. Kino's ingredients really are independent. What's missing isn't an abstraction to enable mixing; it's a way for a look to suggest its grammar, and that can wait until a piece needs it.

05What else changed today

The full review is in ARCHITECTURE_AUDIT.md. The parts you'd notice:

The cyanotype grille was off

A shader variable shadowing bug, introduced by the restructure, made the grille a no-op. Every cyanotype frame since the render you approved had no grille. Fixed: grille strength on screen-test went from 0.8 back to 16.7 (approved: 16.3).

screenPass is 2.5× faster

The screen is now rendered once per pixel and then blurred, instead of re-evaluated inside every blur step: 1595 → 648 ms per frame at 1440p, so a 27-second piece takes about 9 minutes instead of 22. The two versions can't be told apart, even at the worst-differing spot:

Testsnpm test: 26 tests on the frame clock, the music signals, the camera, the print run, the timing helpers. scripts/smoke.sh: end to end on a synthetic composition, and it fails if the grille ever stops working again.
BugsThe first frame was drawn twice per render. A beat grid with repeated beats would have hung a render forever. The bass follower returned NaN on silence. WebGL failures now say which pass failed and why.
